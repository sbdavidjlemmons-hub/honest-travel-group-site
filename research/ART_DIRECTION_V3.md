## Two things that need saying once

**1. Condo interiors carry a disclosure problem the exteriors do not.**
The certificate names no property and assigns the unit at confirmation from a destination
list. A specific, recognizable interior implies a specific unit. Free-stock interiors are
also overwhelmingly listing photography shot for one real property. Working rule for S3:
generic interiors only — no nameplate, no branded linen, no resort-identifying fixture —
and the existing representative-photography disclosure must sit with the back image, not
only on the front. This does not block the work; it constrains which frames qualify.

**2. CRU-CHA cannot be fixed with a photograph.**
Charleston has no cruise service. Carnival's homeport contract ended January 2025, Union
Pier closed to cruise ships 30 June 2026, and the only Charleston departures are 8-night
voyages at $5,990-$7,835 per person against a page advertising $800-$1,800. A better image
makes an undeliverable certificate more persuasive, which is worse, not better. Replacing
the art is held until the certificate is retired or re-pointed.
