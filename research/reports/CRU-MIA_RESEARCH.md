## Risk notes
- **The currently displayed "$700–$2,000" band is not supported.** Eight verified oceanview totals for two adults on 3–5 night PortMiami sailings ran **$592 to $1,066**, with the single disclosed undiscounted retail at $1,210. Nothing in the researched set approaches $2,000 for an outside cabin at these lengths — the band's own floor ($700) is also above the real low ($592). Leaving $2,000 on the page is the single largest legal exposure in this certificate.
- **Single-source pricing.** Every priced row resolves to one retail engine (WMPH Vacations / iCruise.com). Royal Caribbean, Carnival, CruiseSheet, CruiseDirect, GoToSea and VacationsToGo all either blocked the fetch or publish no category fares. A one-engine basis is defensible but thin; a second engine should be added at the next refresh.
- **Fare volatility on identical product.** Rows 5 and 6 are the same ship, same cabin category, five days apart, and differ by $160 for two ($796 vs. $636). Any single displayed number will be wrong for some sailings in both directions; the "research context only" disclaimer is doing real work here and must not be softened.
- **Certificate promises no ship, line, or itinerary.** Every comparable is a specific named ship on a specific date. The comparison is category-to-category (3–5 nights, 2 adults, outside stateroom, PortMiami), not trip-to-trip, and should never be framed as "this exact cruise for $314."
- **Row 1 is unbookable under the certificate's own rules** (2026-10-22 is 43 days out, inside the 45-day notice requirement). It is retained as market context only and its inclusion does not move the recommended figure.
- **Gratuities.** Both sides exclude them, so the comparison holds — but a traveler will pay roughly $16–$20 per person per day on any of these sailings on top of either number. Worth a line of copy so the savings claim is not read as all-in.
- **June–August and holiday blackouts remove the highest-fare weeks** from what the certificate can deliver. This cuts both ways and is disclosed: it means the certificate cannot be compared against peak-summer pricing, and the researched Oct–Nov / Jan–May window is the correct and conservative basis.
- **Image branding.** Both nominated images show an identifiable ship and line. The primary shows Carnival, a line the certificate names; the alternate shows a specific famous Royal Caribbean vessel. The existing on-card line "Line, ship, itinerary, ports and departure date are not guaranteed" must remain visible wherever either image is used.
