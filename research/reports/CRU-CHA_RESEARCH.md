## Risk notes
- **The named port cannot fulfil the named product.** Charleston lost mass-market homeporting in January 2025 (Carnival contract ended) and lost cruise-ship berthing at Union Pier entirely on June 30, 2026. Marketing a "Cruise from Charleston" certificate for 3–5 nights in an outside stateroom describes a trip that is not currently purchasable from that city by anyone, at any price.
- **The live page's existing $800–$1,800 retail comparison is unsupported.** No Charleston sailing at any length or category falls in that band; the only Charleston departures start near $6,000 per person. That number appears to have been derived from a generic short-cruise market rather than from Charleston, and it is the single largest legal exposure on this page.
- **Stale third-party marketing copy will mislead a spot-check.** The Cruise Web still says Charleston "is a significant homeport … most notably Carnival Cruise Line," and Direct Line Cruises still runs a "Carnival Cruises from Charleston" landing page — while both sites' own live inventory returns zero Carnival Charleston departures. Anyone verifying this certificate against SEO pages rather than booking engines will reach the wrong conclusion.
- **The "port not guaranteed" disclaimer is doing all the work.** The page says line, ship, itinerary, ports and departure date are not guaranteed, which is likely the only thing that keeps the certificate fulfillable — the traveler would in practice sail from Jacksonville or elsewhere. But the certificate's own title, headline, H1, meta description, FAQ schema and intent form all say "Charleston," and the intent form's confirmation text reads *"you're on the list for Charleston, South Carolina."* The disclaimer contradicts the entire rest of the page.
- **Small-ship substitution is not equivalent.** American Cruise Lines does still depart Charleston, so a literal-minded reading of "cruise from Charleston" is satisfiable — but at $5,990–$7,835 per person for 8 nights one-way, versus $314 in certificate charges. No operator will honor that gap, so ACL inventory is not a fulfillment path.
- Image note: every approved candidate necessarily shows a large cruise ship berthed at a Charleston terminal that **no longer accepts cruise ships**. The imagery is authentic and correctly located, but it depicts a scene that ended on June 30, 2026. Keep the existing "representative vessel" art note on the card, and prefer the *Hebridean Sky* alternate if the smaller vessel reads as less of an implied promise.
