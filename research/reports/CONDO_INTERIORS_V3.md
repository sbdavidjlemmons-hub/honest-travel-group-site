## The product test applied

The certificate is 7 nights, sleeps 4, with a kitchen. Each frame was required to show at
least two of: a kitchen or kitchenette, a dining surface, a living/sofa zone, a balcony or
view out. A bedroom-only or single-armchair frame fails the product even when it passes every
compliance test — the interior analogue of C4b. This test killed more candidates than branding did.

## 6. Vocabulary addendum — for the remaining 15 interiors

Record these so the next sweep is cheaper. They are new, and they invert part of C6.

**I1. `interior` is a searchable word; `condo`, `villa` and `vacation rental` still are not.**
The brief's standing ban holds, but the replacement vocabulary works well.

**I2. THE WORD `apartment` PULLS EXTERIORS WHEN PAIRED WITH A LANDSCAPE NOUN.**
`tropical apartment balcony palm` on Pexels returned **24 building facades and zero interiors**.
`apartment` is to interiors what `hotel` is to destinations in C6 — a solvent. Unsplash
`apartment-interior` works but is **5-in-20 Unsplash+**, the worst paywall density seen.

**I3. The two query shapes that actually return interiors:**
- `<landscape noun> + interior` — `lake house interior`, `mountain cabin interior`
- `<interior noun> + <view noun>` — `balcony sea view`, `beach house living room ocean`,
  `cozy cabin living room fireplace`

The *view* word is what drags an interior toward a destination. `beach house living room ocean`
returned **20 interiors in 24 results** and produced the Cancun approval; `lake house interior`
returned **~22 landscape-orientation interiors in 24** and is the single highest-yield interior
query found.

**I4. ORIENTATION IS A HARD FILTER — CHECK IT BEFORE FETCHING.** Interiors are shot portrait far
more often than landscapes are. In the `cozy cabin living room fireplace` set, **6 of the 8
strongest candidates were 2:3 portrait** and cannot make 1600x900 without gutting the
composition. Read the orientation off the search page and skip portraits; it saves multi-megabyte
fetches, the same economy C6b buys by reading the location field.

**I5. THE INTERIOR BRAND TARGET LIST IS DIFFERENT FROM THE EXTERIOR ONE.** Exterior sweeps
magnify facades and low retail signage (C3b). Interiors fail on **small objects**: appliance
badges, coffee machines, TV bezels, book spines, bottle labels, shelf maker's stamps, toiletries,
remote controls. Four of the five findings in this sweep were of that class and **none** was a
property nameplate. An agent who sweeps the walls and not the countertop will ship a branded
frame.

**I6. SUPPLY IS ABUNDANT AND PLAUSIBILITY IS THE BINDING CONSTRAINT — the reverse of C2.**
C2 says destination supply is singular-or-none. Interiors invert it: there are thousands, and
the scarce thing is one that reads as the right *place*. Two of four approvals here came down to
matching forest type and architectural vernacular, not to finding an unbranded room.

**I7. Free interior stock skews hard to Europe, Türkiye, Scandinavia and the Indian Ocean.**
European wall sockets, panel radiators, roller shutters, Alpine stone-and-timber and Swahili
decor were the most common plausibility failures. US-reading interiors are the genuinely scarce
subset — which is why the two American-located finds (Swan Lake NY, Evergreen CO) were worth
chasing through Unsplash's location field.

## 7. Handling rules for publication

  filenames, photographer descriptions or location strings may reach the site. "Swan Lake, NY",

  `alt` text and filenames.

  front. These are interiors; the implication of a specific unit is stronger here than on any
  exterior, and the certificate assigns the unit at confirmation.
- **CON-CUN: the full frame is prohibited.** Only the crop box at §4 may be published.
- **CON-WID: the full frame is prohibited.** Only the crop box at §3 may be published — the
  uncropped original carries a legible Nescafé Dolce Gusto logo.
