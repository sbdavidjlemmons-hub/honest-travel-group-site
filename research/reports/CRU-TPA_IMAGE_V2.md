### What I actually see in this image

A long-lens (300 mm) view across lower Tampa Bay at sunset. The **Sunshine Skyway Bridge** runs the
full width of the frame as a near-black silhouette — both cable-stayed pylons, the fan of stays, the
low approach trestles and the concrete dolphins/fenders standing off each pier. A **cruise ship is
transiting the main navigation channel**, framed dead-centre between the two pylons, deck lights on,
against a banded orange-and-rose sky. Water below is a flat, even, unbroken slate-blue.

**Ship size in frame.** The ship measures **≈ 500 px of a 6,016 px frame — 8.3 % of frame width.**
Far under the ~40 % ceiling; in the 1600 × 900 delivery it is ~133 px wide. The port, not the ship,
is the subject — as the rule demands.

**Legible ship name?** **No.** I cropped the ship out of the native file at 1:1 (native box
2412, 2021 → 3055, 2475) and re-opened it, then again at 3× (`bs_ship_3x.jpg`). At 3× the transom
carries a dark smear where a name plate would sit; it does **not** resolve into letters — it reads as
sensor noise at ISO 6400. Nothing on the hull side, nothing on the bow.

**Legible line wordmark?** **No.** The funnel is an undifferentiated dark block. No crown-and-anchor,
no whale tail, no MSC monogram, no Carnival red. A single small blue glow near the mast is a deck
light, not a logo. At 3× there is no readable trade dress anywhere on the vessel.

**Third-party commercial signage?** **None.** I tiled the whole horizon band at native 1:1
(y 1400–2700, in three 2000 px tiles — `bs_tile0/1/2.jpg`) and opened each. Contents: bridge deck,
pylons, stays, trestle piers, pier-protection dolphins, a faint Fort De Soto shoreline at the far left,
water, sky. No billboards, no rooftop signs, no vehicle liveries, no port company names. Zero text
in the frame.

**Vibe test.** Passes emphatically. Golden-hour silhouette, a lit ship heading to sea, a landmark
bridge. It reads as *departure*, which is precisely the emotional beat a cruise certificate wants.
No cranes, no silos, no car parks, no razor wire, no refuse.

**Overlay space.** Exceptional. The top ~45 % is clean graded sky; the bottom ~45 % is flat, texture-
free water. Either half takes translucent type without a single competing detail.

### Deviation from the "port evidence" row — declared

The brief's composition table asks for terminal / gantry / mooring dolphins / gangway / parking
structure / second berthed vessel in frame. **This frame has none of those** — it is a transit shot,
not a berth shot. I am nominating it anyway because the coordinator's own tasking for CRU-TPA states:
*"A ship transiting Tampa Bay with the Skyway behind it would satisfy both rules cleanly — look for
that specifically."* The Skyway substitutes for berth infrastructure as the destination anchor, and it
anchors harder: the Skyway is unmistakably Tampa Bay in a way no cruise terminal building is.
Partial credit on the literal row: the bridge's **pier-protection dolphins** are marine channel
infrastructure and are visible across the frame.

**Port evidence.** Satisfied properly — a working berth with loading arms and bollards, a shore crane,

**Vibe test.** Passes. Bright, high-key, a big Florida sky, a white ship leaving a real city. It sells
a departure from Tampa specifically, which is more than the generic-harbour frames on Pexels can do.

**Industrial content — assessed against the Tampa warning.** The brief warns that Tampa's cruise
berths sit beside phosphate and bulk facilities. This frame does include a **petroleum tank farm and a
shore crane** on the right-hand shore. They occupy the right ~12 % of the frame, sit low, are read as
a dark band behind the ship at delivery scale, and are the kind of *berth* infrastructure the brief
explicitly permits ("gantry"). This is nothing like the rejected Port Canaveral panorama, where
gantries and silos were the subject and ships were specks — here a white cruise ship is unambiguously
the second subject after the skyline.

### THE CAVEAT — third-party signage on the distant skyline (disclosed, not hidden)

I cropped the skyline out of the **native** file 1:1 (`f6_skyline.jpg`, native box 180, 2124 →
2250, 2664) and opened it. At native the downtown rooftops carry:

| Mark | Where | Width at native | Width in the 1600 × 900 delivery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SunTrust** | crown of 401 E Jackson (right of centre) | ~79 px | ~37 px (≈ 4.6 px per glyph) |
| **PNC** (red logotype) | crown of Rivergate Tower, the round one | ~40 px | ~19 px |
| "Wells Fargo" | mid-rise, very low contrast | ~55 px | ~25 px |
| "Tampa Convention Center" | facade band, very low contrast | ~90 px | ~42 px |

**At native these resolve; "SunTrust" is plainly readable.** At the 1600 × 900 delivery I rendered
and opened, none of them resolve — "SunTrust" is a pale smudge on a building crown at ~4.6 px per
character. **I am reporting both numbers rather than picking the flattering one**, because that is
what the 100 % rule exists to prevent.

**Judgement, stated plainly:** the brief's brand row bars "third-party commercial signage (malls,
shipping lines, hotels)". A downtown skyline with bank names on tower crowns is a different animal
from the "OUTLET COLLECTION RIVERWALK" mall sign that helped sink the New Orleans candidate — that
mark was large, frontal and legible at delivery size; these are 4–5 px-per-glyph rooftop marks in the
far distance. **This cannot be reframed away**: SunTrust sits at native x ≈ 1384 with PNC at x ≈ 395,
so any crop that excludes them also excludes the skyline that makes the image Tampa, and pushes the
ship from 20 % to ~65 % of frame width — which breaks the more important rule. I tested it; it makes
the image worse. So the caveat stands as a caveat. **If the coordinator reads the signage row
strictly, this alternate falls and the certificate ships on the primary alone.** I would rather say
that than bury the finding.

## Licence risk — read before publishing the primary

**The primary is CC BY-SA 4.0.** The brief flags share-alike as a caution because it attaches to any
crop published. Practically, for CRU-TPA:

- Attribution line required, e.g. *"Photo: Kenneth John Gill (Gillfoto), CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons."*
- The **cropped 1600 × 900 image itself** is an adaptation and must be offered under CC BY-SA 4.0.
- Share-alike attaches to the image, **not** to the page it sits on or to Honest Travel Group's own
  copy elsewhere on the site. Hero images under CC BY-SA are routinely published this way; it is a
  labelling obligation, not a contamination risk.
- **If the coordinator wants no share-alike at all**, the ordering flips: the alternate
  (*Florida-6*, CC BY 2.0, attribution only) or the reframe recovery (Pexels Licence, no attribution
