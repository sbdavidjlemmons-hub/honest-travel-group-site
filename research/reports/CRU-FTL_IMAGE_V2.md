### What I actually see in this image

Wide view across the Port Everglades turning basin from the water. **Three passenger cruise ships lie
at their berths in the middle distance**, left to right: a Royal Caribbean Vision-class bow, a dark-hulled
Holland America Vista-class, and a Princess Sun-class. In front of and between them: the cruise
terminal buildings with teal and turquoise roof trim, a row of palms, a working tug on a barge, a red
channel buoy, two small pleasure boats, and — at the right edge — the multi-storey Port Everglades
parking structure.

**Ship size in frame.** Left ship ≈ 15 % of frame width, centre ship ≈ 30 %, right ship ≈ 21 %. All are
comfortably under the ~40 % ceiling. No single ship is the subject; the port is.

**Legible ship name?** No. At full frame and at 1200 px delivery width, nothing is readable. Under a
2.5× crop the Holland America stern shows a faint white-on-navy smudge (it is *Zuiderdam / Rotterdam*)
that still cannot be read. Under a 2.3× crop the Princess bow shows a light-grey-on-white mark that
resolves only at that magnification. At the size this will ever be published, both are texture, not text.

**Legible line wordmark?** No. The Holland America funnel carries its small circular emblem — an
abstract mark, no text, and tiny. No Celebrity "X", no Carnival whale-tail, no Royal Caribbean
crown-and-anchor is visible anywhere in the frame.

**Third-party commercial signage?** None. I zoomed the terminal frontage, the parking structure and
the quay: no mall names, no shipping-line names, no hotel names, no advertising.

**Freight risk.** Clean. No container stacks, no container gantries, no tank farm, no cement silos.
One modest pier crane sits at the right edge, small and unobtrusive. This reads as a cruise terminal,
not a container yard — which matters, because most Port Everglades photography does not.

**Overlay space.** Excellent. The top ~35 % is unbroken pale sky; the bottom ~40 % is unbroken water.
Either band takes a translucent hero or header block without competing with detail.

**Vibe test.** Yes. Bright Florida light, palm trees, turquoise-trimmed terminals, pleasure boats on
blue water. It sells a vacation.

**Caveat to flag:** 2,048 × 1,536 is modest. It is fine for a card hero or a header at 1× and 2×
on a standard card, but it will not survive a full-bleed 2560 px hero. Crop from the 4:3 original to
whatever aspect the certificate card needs — the licence permits it freely.

### What I actually see in this image

Dusk at the Port Everglades entrance, shot from the beach. A Princess Grand-class ship is transiting
the channel behind the north jetty, lit up, backlit by a lavender-and-rose afterglow. A second ship is
a dark silhouette on the horizon at right. Foreground: wet sand, a curving tide line catching pink
reflected light, and a rock groyne running into the surf.

**Legible ship name?** No — not at full frame, not at 1200 px, not at 1.7× zoom. The bow name is an
unresolvable dark smudge on white.

**Legible line wordmark?** No text. The Princess "seawitch" device appears on the funnel spoiler as a
small blue abstract shape, roughly 3 % of the ship's length and high in the superstructure. It is the
same order of exposure as the small "MSC" the coordinator accepted on the Port Canaveral sunset.

**Third-party commercial signage?** None anywhere in frame.

**Freight risk.** None. No cranes, no containers, no tanks, no industrial structures at all.

**Overlay space.** Very good. The top ~35 % is a smooth pastel gradient with no detail — arguably a
better overlay ground than the primary's. The bottom ~40 % is beach; it is clean sand and tide line,
though with scattered natural seaweed wrack rather than a swept resort beach.

**Vibe test.** Strong. This is the most beautiful frame of everything reviewed, and it reads
unmistakably as departure-eve.

### Two disclosed deviations from the V2 formula

1. **Ship occupies ≈ 48 % of frame width**, over the ~40 % guideline. Mitigation: because the ship sits
   in the upper-left quadrant behind the jetty, a crop that lifts the horizon or extends right reduces
   the proportion; and the ship is a lit distant silhouette, not a legible subject.
2. **Berth infrastructure is a channel jetty and groyne, not a terminal, gantry, dolphin, gangway,
   parking structure or second *berthed* vessel.** The second ship on the horizon is underway, not moored.
   The jetty unambiguously reads as a port entrance, but it is not one of the six listed proofs.

Neither deviation touches the brand test, which is the failure mode that killed the previous nominees.
If the coordinator wants a strictly-conforming alternate, the primary is the only file in this survey
that satisfies every criterion without qualification, and there is no second one.

## Notes for the coordinator

**Why Fort Lauderdale is harder than the other ports.** Port Everglades cruise berths sit directly
opposite the Southport container terminal, the CEMEX/Continental cement silos and the petroleum tank
farm. Any wide shot from the north or west side of the basin picks up freight; any tight shot picks up
a hull name. The two approvals both resolve this the same way the V2 brief predicts — by shooting
across the water at the *cruise* side of the basin (primary), or by leaving the basin entirely and
shooting the inlet at dusk (alternate).
