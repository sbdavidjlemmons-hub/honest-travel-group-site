### What I actually see in this image

A telephoto view **down over Honolulu from Tantalus Heights at sunset**. A coconut-palm frond fills
the lower-left corner in silhouette. Below and to the right: the Ala Moana / Kakaʻako high-rise

line of palms, and open ocean running out to a soft hazy horizon. The sun sits half-set in the
upper-right quadrant. **Above and to the right of the sun, a twin-engine widebody airliner is climbing
away in silhouette**, seen from below and behind.

**Aircraft.** Real, exterior, in flight — not a cabin window, not a wing. It spans roughly **250 px of
5,191**, i.e. **≈ 4.8 % of frame width**, centred at about 68 % across and 16 % down.

**Legible airline name?** No. I cropped the aircraft at 4× native magnification (a 550 × 260 px region

file supports.

**Third-party commercial signage?** None. I zoomed the entire skyline band (a 2300 × 950 px region at
native resolution) and the lower-left corner. Every tower is an anonymous residential/hotel slab. The

**Freight / industrial risk.** Clean. No cranes, no container stacks, no silos, no tank farm. One

## Findings for the coordinator

**1. Free-licence sources CAN meet this standard for Honolulu — but only via the destination, never
via the aircraft.** Every single frame in which the aeroplane was the photographic subject failed:
Hawaiian at OGG, Delta at HNL, US Airways at HNL, Cathay on approach, Virgin between palms, American
Trans Air at dusk. Six for six. The two approvals are both frames where **the photographer was
shooting Oʻahu and an aeroplane happened to be in the sky.** That is the airfare analogue of the
cruise rule: **shoot the destination, not the aeroplane.** Recommend adding it to the brief.

**2. The V2 "distance / backlight / silhouette" logic works, and it is the only thing that works.**
Every approved and reserve frame is backlit at golden hour or dusk with the aircraft at ≤ 15 % of
frame width. Every rejection on branding was a sunlit aircraft at ≥ 30 % of frame width. The
correlation was total across 24 files.

**3. The trade the coordinator now has to make explicitly.** There is a genuine tension between
*Hawaiʻi-authentic* and *aeroplane-prominent*:
- **Hawaiʻi + anonymous aircraft → aircraft is small.** (Primary and Alternate. Both real Oʻahu.)
- **Aeroplane-prominent + anonymous → not Hawaiʻi.** (Reserve R1 Bali, R2 Palm Springs.)
- **Hawaiʻi + aeroplane-prominent → livery legible.** (Rejection #5, the Delta 767 over Diamond Head.)
No free-licence file was found that resolves all three. I have nominated from the first group because
the brief prioritises destination connection and prohibits identifiable livery absolutely.

**4. Practical note on the Wikimedia 429 that other agents will hit.** `upload.wikimedia.org` blocked
this session for roughly twenty minutes even with the prescribed User-Agent and exponential backoff.
Two workarounds proved reliable and are worth adding to the brief:
- **`WebFetch` against `commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:…` was never rate-limited**, and returns
  complete file and subcategory listings — use it instead of the `action=query` API for discovery.
- **Most Commons aviation and travel files are Flickr imports, and the Flickr photo ID is embedded in
  the Commons filename** (e.g. `Plane landing at HNL on a voggy afternoon. (26355990245).jpg`).
  Searching the Openverse API (`https://api.openverse.org/v1/images/?q=…`, anonymous, `page_size` max
  20) returns the `live.staticflickr.com` URL for the same file, which downloads normally while
  Wikimedia is blocked — and Flickr usually offers **larger sizes than Commons holds**.
- Flickr's `_k` / `_h` / `_o` size suffixes use a **different secret** from `_b`; guessing them returns
  HTTP 410. Fetch `…/sizes/o/` (or `/sizes/k/`) via WebFetch to get the real URL. This is how the
  primary went from 1,024 px to 5,191 px and the alternate from 1,024 px to 4,875 px.

**5. Licence hygiene.** Both nominations are **plain CC BY 2.0** — commercial use permitted,
attribution only, no share-alike, no NoDerivatives, crops unencumbered. No CC BY-SA file was
nominated. The one CC BY-SA candidate encountered (#11) was rejected on other grounds anyway.
